<Sec label="Context" title="Clients were filling out a form that was written for someone else.">

Clients in some markets, mostly in Europe, use Microflex, a ticketing program that doesn't integrate with Ticketmaster's main one. This form is how they send event details to our Event Programmers (EPs), and it lands in Salesforce like a support case.

The form was structured like the Salesforce backend, so clients saw a pile of inputs that only EPs needed, and many of them couldn't be filled in by a client at all.

</Sec>

<Sec label="The problem" title="Easier for clients to fill out, without losing what EPs need.">

*How might we make the event intake form easier for clients to fill out, while maintaining accuracy for Event Programmers and optimizing the event creation workflow?*

The aim was faster, more accurate event builds for EPs. That meant fewer errors, less back and forth, and a better experience for clients and employees alike.

</Sec>

<Sec label="Where I started" title="Before redesigning, I audited every field in the old form.">

The old flow was seven pages, not counting pop-ups. To decide what a client-facing form actually needs, I laid every screen out on one board and went through the inputs one by one, asking whether a client could answer it and whether the evidence said it belonged.

<Frame src="/protected-media/case-studies/event-programming-form/old-form-audit.webp" alt="The old event request flow laid out screen by screen, with research notes and red markup showing what to cut" width="3000" height="1124" />

I took out the inputs clients couldn't fill in, because only an EP knows the answer. Earlier user research and field usage data pointed to more changes:

<Evidence>
<Ev title="Rarely used fields">Salesforce field usage data showed which fields were rarely filled in, and those came out.</Ev>
<Ev title="Internal Salesforce names">Headers like "TM Event Request" cluttered the page and stayed in English in the localized versions.</Ev>
<Ev title="Validation only on submit">Research asked for inline validation, plus help tooltips on fields that need an explanation.</Ev>
</Evidence>

Every field that came out had a reason on record.

</Sec>

<Sec label="Principles" title="Guiding principles">

<Principles>
<Pr title="Ask clients what clients know">

If only an EP can answer it, it doesn't belong on the client's form.

</Pr>
<Pr title="Show a field only when it matters">

The form changes with the answer: single or multi-day, GA or reserved seating, presale or not.

</Pr>
</Principles>

</Sec>

<Sec label="Step 1" title="Event Info">

<Lead>Three pages became one.</Lead>

Event Info, Presale Details and Venue Selection now share one page. Single or multi-day is a radio button, not the toggle I first designed, to fit what Salesforce can build.

<Frame tag="Before and iterations" src="/protected-media/case-studies/event-programming-form/step-1-before.webp" alt="The original Event Request form beside three mockup iterations of the Event Info page" width="3600" height="1213" />

<Frame tag="After" video="/protected-media/case-studies/event-programming-form/step-1.mp4" poster="/protected-media/case-studies/event-programming-form/step-1-poster.jpg" />

<Principles>
<Pr title="Presale: it belongs here">

EP leads said presale fits better with event info than with ticketing, so it moved here as a yes or no.

</Pr>
<Pr title="Venue list: full list stays">

I wanted it filtered to each client's own venues. The BA said it is a picklist in Salesforce, so the full searchable list stays.

</Pr>
</Principles>

</Sec>

<Sec label="Step 2" title="Venue and Ticket Details">

<Lead>Two pages and a pop-up became one page.</Lead>

The most important part of the form, and the hardest to fill out. The old pages had weak hierarchy, blocked-out fields and an overwhelming layout. My first iterations had only a few fields, based on what clients were filling in today. After talking with EP leads, I added blockings and resales, and split venue layout from ticket details.

<Frame tag="Before and iterations" src="/protected-media/case-studies/event-programming-form/step-2-before.webp" alt="The three original pages (Venue Details, Inventory Configuration and Pricing, Price type pop-up) above three mockup iterations of the ticket page" width="3600" height="2147" />

<Frame tag="After" video="/protected-media/case-studies/event-programming-form/step-2.mp4" poster="/protected-media/case-studies/event-programming-form/step-2-poster.jpg" />

<Principles>
<Pr title="Fees: basic first">

The EP lead in Germany wanted eight fees up front per ticket category, while leadership wanted only the basics. I landed on an Add Fee link, an apply-to-all checkbox and a total price, so clients add what's needed and check their own math.

</Pr>
<Pr title="Dynamic pricing: is it needed?">

The EP leads split on whether it belonged on the form. The EP lead in the Netherlands suggested asking the event promoters, which showed me how many live event layers each detail touches.

</Pr>
</Principles>

</Sec>

<Sec label="Step 3" title="Additional Details, then review">

<Lead>A short last page, then Final Review before anything is sent.</Lead>

<Frame tag="After" video="/protected-media/case-studies/event-programming-form/step-3.mp4" poster="/protected-media/case-studies/event-programming-form/step-3-poster.jpg" />

<Frame tag="Final Review" video="/protected-media/case-studies/event-programming-form/step-4.mp4" poster="/protected-media/case-studies/event-programming-form/step-4-poster.jpg" />

<Principles>
<Pr title="Supporting documents: keep or cut">

The EP lead in the Netherlands suggested dropping the upload. Other EPs said client uploads help with ticket-related requests, so it stayed.

</Pr>
<Pr title="Review: ideal, if possible">

The EP lead in the Netherlands asked for a review step, so I designed one, flagged as ideal if Salesforce can build it.

</Pr>
</Principles>

</Sec>

<Sec label="Impact" title="Success metrics to track.">

These are the measures the project set up to track once clients test the first draft build.

<Principles>
<Pr title="Email">

A 15% drop in email between onboarded clients and EPs in the first year.

</Pr>
<Pr title="Adoption">

More event requests arriving through the form than by email.

</Pr>
<Pr title="Speed">

Less time to complete a case, and lower customer effort.

</Pr>
<Pr title="Satisfaction">

A 5 point NPS gain, better CSAT, and higher employee satisfaction.

</Pr>
</Principles>

</Sec>

<Sec label="Reflection" title="Two sides to every problem.">

<Lesson title="Design is all about tradeoffs.">

Clients shouldn't be bombarded with too much information, but the business still needs accurate details and an efficient workflow. Every field was a tradeoff between the two, and I learned the best designs come from weighing both instead of picking a side.

</Lesson>

<Lesson title="My first form this complex.">

It was my first time designing a form with this many markets and edge cases. Every interaction also had to be simple enough to build in Salesforce, so I learned to design around what the platform can support.

</Lesson>

</Sec>
