<Intro>
Dean Unnava of the UC Davis Graduate School of Management, committed to supporting the lifelong professional success of alumni, aimed to extend community and educational access through a centralized platform.

The GSM could offer alumni:

- Professional certificates
- GSM Course content

Before designing any of it, we needed to understand how alumni actually approach learning, support, and staying connected after graduating.
</Intro>

<Section label="Core Themes">

Synthesis pulled the research into three themes.

<div className="caption-block">
<p className="label">Community & Support</p>

Alumni want transparent communication and to feel genuinely engaged after graduating, not just reached out to when it's convenient.
</div>

<div className="caption-block">
<p className="label">Networking & Mentorship</p>

Alumni want easier ways to connect professionally beyond their existing circles, for mentorship, jobs, and industry ties.
</div>

<div className="caption-block">
<p className="label">Learning</p>

Alumni want credible, time-efficient content that keeps them competitive and current.
</div>

These three became the platform's pillars, and every feature had to earn its place against one of them.

</Section>

<Section label="The Opportunity">

Our research revealed that the core need goes beyond learning alone:

<div className="content-block">
### Learning alone isn't the core issue

<p className="hmw">→ How might we design a platform that prioritizes community, professional development, and meaningful alumni connection — not just access to content?</p>
</div>

<div className="content-block">
### Current alumni support is limited

<p className="hmw">→ How might we empower alumni to support one another and co-create a stronger, self-sustaining network?</p>
</div>

<div className="content-block">
### We're not the first lifelong learning platform

<p className="hmw">→ How might we differentiate by creating a holistic alumni experience that blends high-quality content with real connection and career growth?</p>
</div>

<ProblemBox>
How might we create a centralized platform where alumni and students can access high-quality learning content, engage in meaningful professional conversations, and continue to foster a strong, supportive GSM community for lifelong growth?
</ProblemBox>

</Section>

At mid-fi, I added branding, copy, and interaction detail to prep the prototype for alumni testing in Figma, ensuring it was clear and engaging for participants unfamiliar with UX workflows. The goal: focused feedback on key features.

<Section label="Usability Testing">

We tested the mid-fi prototype with alumni and rebuilt the areas that tripped them up. Three of the problems ran deeper than a single screen, and each one turned into a structural decision.

<div className="content-block">
### Search had no clear purpose

Was it for people or content? I split it: a focused search on the learning page, and a top nav pointing directly to the directory and forum.
</div>

<div className="content-block">
### Saved and published content had no home

I added a bookmark icon linking to saved content, and moved uploading onto the learning homepage, grouping every learning action in one place.
</div>

<div className="content-block">
### The geomap was buried in advanced search

I moved it to a single map-expansion button, front and center.
</div>

The pairings below show each screen before testing and the version we shipped, with the reasoning called out.

</Section>

This project stretched my thinking, sharpened my skills, and made me even more excited to grow as a product designer.

<div className="content-block">
### What challenged me was prototyping limits in Figma

Creating an advanced filter system pushed Figma to its edge. Without conditionals or multiple actions, I couldn't fully simulate the real interaction, which impacted usability testing.
</div>

<div className="content-block">
### I learned that PMs are magic

Watching our PMs in action was eye-opening. From organizing roadmaps in Notion to feature scoping and aligning the team, I left this project with a deep respect for cross-functional collaboration.
</div>

<div className="content-block">
### Accomplishments
